In 1962, two mathematicians proved something that sounds like a dating cheat code: when everyone ranks everyone, there is a way to pair people up so that nobody has a reason to run off together. The twist? The side that makes the first move gets the best partner it could get. The side that waits gets the worst.

This is the algorithm of love: how it works, why it won a Nobel Prize, and what it really says about who should make the first move. Plus why a dating app can feel like a casino, and when to stop searching.
